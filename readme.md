# Beamforming Based on Deep Learning for Large-Scale Antenna Array Design [Attached Relevant Code]

Abstract: In the context of limited radio frequency chains and analog beamforming structures based on phase shifters, the design of beamforming (BF) for large-scale antenna arrays has been recognized as a critical issue in millimeter wave communication systems due to the challenges posed by imperfect channel state information (CSI). In this letter, we propose a deep learning-based BF design method and develop a Beamforming Neural Network (BFNN) that can be trained to learn how to optimize the beamformer to maximize spectral efficiency while accounting for hardware limitations and imperfect CSI. Simulation results demonstrate that this algorithm significantly improves performance compared to traditional BF algorithms, demonstrating strong robustness against imperfect CSI.
Index Terms - Deep Learning (DL), Millimeter Wave (mmWave), Beamforming (BF) Design, Large-Scale Antenna Arrays, Neural Networks (NN), Beamforming Neural Network (BFNN).
I. Introduction
A. Background and Motivation
Recently, the hybrid analog-digital beamforming (HBF) design for millimeter wave (mmWave) communication systems with large antenna arrays has gained significant attention due to its ability to provide high beamforming (BF) gain to compensate for severe path loss at acceptable hardware costs and power consumption [1] [2]. It is recognized that the most challenging aspect of HBF optimization problems is the constant modulus constraint on the BF, which is due to its phased array architecture [3]-[5]. In existing work addressing this difficult problem using model-based design methods, an algorithm based on Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) was proposed in [1]. However, analog beamformers are limited to predefined codebooks.To enhance the performance of OMP, in [3] and [4], manifold optimization methods were applied to simulate blast furnace optimization. In [5], a unit-by-unit iterative algorithm was proposed for optimizing the simulated beamformer. However, all these algorithms either require some approximations to simplify the original objective function or require extensive serial time-consuming iterations to obtain solutions. Moreover, perfect channel state information (CSI) assumptions are made in all these algorithms.
In another aspect, recent work on intelligent communication has shown the tremendous potential of data-driven deep learning (DL) methods in addressing traditional challenging problems [6]-[9], [11]. Inspired by these works, in this letter we aim to apply DL methods to tackle complex mmWave system BFR design issues, hardware constraints, and imperfect CSI. Our motivation can be explained from three aspects:

·Firstly, it is well known that due to the BFR design being a rather complex non-convex problem, finding an explicit closed-form solution is unlikely because of the joint optimization of multiple variables and constant modulus constraints [1]. Given that DL has been recognized as an effective method for tackling tough problems [12], what if we use DL to solve the BFR optimization problem? What would be interesting.

·Secondly, based on extensive training iterations with large amounts of samples, DL-based solutions have been proven to possess the ability to understand the complex characteristics of wireless channels [6]. Compared to conventional works assuming perfect CSI [1], [3][5], DL-based methods are expected to have strong robustness against imperfect CSI.

·Most effective traditional BFR algorithms require high-complexity time-consuming serial iterations [4], [5]. However, neural networks (NN) trained offline have limited matrix multiplication and addition complexity when deployed online. Moreover, due to the acceleration of parallel computing, DL-based schemes can operate quickly, making them more suitable for high-speed communications.

Recently, there have been some works on DL-based HBF design [13][14]. However, in [13] the assumption of ideal CSI was made, and the output beamformer did not directly satisfy the constant modulus constraint. In [14], the simulated beamformer was limited to predefined codebooks, which often leads to certain performance losses.
B. Novelty and Contribution
In this letter, we propose a DL-based BF design method and develop an BF neural network (BFNN) to train how to optimize the beamformer to maximize spectral efficiency (SE) with hardware constraints and imperfect CSI. The contributions can be summarized as follows:
·New Design Method: Since the analog beamformer is implemented using analog phase shifters, we cannot follow the traditional full-digital design method [6], [7] to replace the analog beamformer with a digital neural network and train it throughout the communication link. Instead, we propose a new DL-based design method by developing an BFNN that directly outputs the optimized beamformer based on the estimated CSI input.
·Novel Loss Function: Unlike traditional NNs where the loss function is typically defined as the mean square error between the transmitted symbols (labels) and the recovered symbols, in the BFNN, we propose an elegant loss function closely related to SE performance.
·Robustness to Imperfect CSI: We present a two-stage design method that makes the BFNN robust to imperfect CSI. In the first offline training stage, the BFNN learns to approach the ideal SE with only actual channel estimates as its input. By doing so, in the second online deployment stage, the BFNN can adapt to imperfect CSI and achieve robust performance against channel estimation errors.

III. Design of DL Model and BFNN
In this section, we first introduce the new challenges in applying DL methods to solve (3) with imperfect CSI. Then, we describe the architecture of BFNN in detail. Finally, we analyze its complexity.
A. Challenge
Due to the specific architecture of the analog beamformer, which includes an analog phase shifter, we cannot follow traditional methods to replace it with a multi-layer neural network (NN) and train it in the BS-user communication link [6]-[8]. Here, we propose a different DL design method by designing a BFNN that directly outputs vRF. However, this design is not trivial due to three challenges.
· What should be the input for BFNN? Most existing works based on DL [6], [7], [14] receive baseband digital signals as inputs. However, this approach cannot be applied here because the received signal itself is a function of an analog pre-coder to be optimized. Moreover, the dimensionality of the received signal is much lower than that of the pre-coder to be optimized (Nt).
· How to ensure that the output (vRF) meets constraints? It is well known that most DL frameworks are not very supportive of complex outputs (e.g., TensorFlow, PyTorch), and further constraining the output with a constant modulus becomes even more challenging.
1. What should the labels be during training of BFNN? In almost all traditional intelligent communication design [8] and [11], the labels are precisely set to the bits sent or perfect CSI. However, it is difficult to find an appropriate label for this BF design problem. For example, if we use an optimized simulated beamformer based on traditional algorithms as the label, the performance of the BFNN will not be better than the traditional algorithm.

In this section, we first discuss some considerations for the above three challenges, then describe the two-stage design method of BFNN.
1) Three specific considerations:
· The input of BFNN: Since the simulated beamformer is implemented using analog components, it cannot be replaced by a fully digital neural network or trained throughout the entire communication link. 2) Two-Stage Design Method: Based on the above considerations for BFNN, Figure 2 illustrates the two-stage BF design method based on DL. In the offline training phase, channel samples are generated through simulation according to the system model in Section 2, pilot symbols are sent, and noise samples are obtained. Subsequently, a practical millimeter wave channel estimator is applied to the BS to obtain partial CSI. In this letter, we apply the classic millimeter wave channel estimator proposed in [2], where the BS estimates the channel by sending beamformed pilot symbols in a hierarchical codebook and using their received signals rP to feedback user decisions. The channel estimate hest and SNR estimate γest are sent as inputs to the BFNN.Note that, as described in Section 2, assuming γest = γ, the BFNN generates the optimized simulated beamforming vector vRFn by minimizing the loss function defined in (5). Since the channel samples and SNR values are generated through simulation, they can be directly used for loss computation, as shown in Figure 2. Due to the nature of deep learning-based techniques being essentially a gradient descent method, it is ensured that the loss converges to a local optimum with an appropriate learning rate [12]. By incorporating channel estimation as input to the BFNN and using perfect CSI in the loss function, the BFNN can learn how to approach the ideal SE with perfect CSI as closely as possible, while also being robust to channel estimation errors.  
During the online deployment phase, the same millimeter-wave channel estimator is applied to the BS for partial CSI acquisition. The channel estimation is then sent to the BFNN to obtain an optimized beamformer. It is important to note that perfect CSI can be calculated only during offline training phases. During online deployment, all parameters of the BFNN are fixed, and the trained BFNN accepts imperfect CSI as input and directly outputs a simulated beamformer without the need for perfect CSI to calculate losses.

C. Complexity Analysis
In this section, we analyze the number of floating-point operations (FLOPs) for the suggested number of BFNNs in the offline training phase. Since the complexity of the offline training phase is not typically included [8], only the complexity during the online deployment phase is considered. According to [16], the FLOP count for dense layers is given by (2NI - 1)NO, where NI and NO represent the input and output dimensions, respectively. For Nt = 64, considering Table 1, the total FLOPs for BFNNs are approximately 150,000.
For traditional model-based HBF designs [1][4][5], the asymptotic computational complexity for the number of complex multiplications is O(N3 t <$N3 t <$N), as they involve operations such as singular value decomposition and matrix inversion. Even if the coefficients in the complexity order are set to 1, with Nt = 64, the number of complex multiplications for N3 t is approximately 260,000. It can be seen that the proposed BFNN has competitive computational complexity compared to traditional model-based HBF algorithms. Moreover, the main operations of BFNN involve large-scale matrix multiplication and addition, which can be effectively accelerated through graphics processing units (GPU). However, most traditional model-based HBF algorithms usually involve sequential iterations (optimization of the next iteration depends on the results of the previous one), and are not suitable for parallel computing.

Discussion on the universality of BFNN
Although BFNN is specifically designed for a simple scenario, in this paper, it demonstrates good generality and can be applied to more complex problems. For example, in the broadband scenario, by cascade multi-tap channel vectors as input and redefine the loss function with a loss function related to broadband SE [4], BFNN can be extended to optimize the analog beamformer of broadband mmWave channels. Another example is considering HBF problems with multiple RF chains. Although the optimal digital beamformer can be solved using closed-form solutions [3] [5], the current simple extension of BFNN is to increase the output dimension of the Nt × NRF analog BF matrix from Nt to NRFNt, with a new loss function. Following a similar thought process, BFNN can also be considered in joint transmit and receive BF design or multi-user BF design.

VI. Conclusion and Future Work
We propose a BF design method based on deep learning for mmWave systems with large antenna arrays. Through special design of custom Lambda layers and loss functions, the algorithm can effectively address the challenges of hardware limitations and imperfect CSI in mmWave systems. Simulation results show that the competitive performance of BFNN and provide valuable insights for future BF design.
As for future work, since BFNN's universality, it is interesting to extend BFNN to more complex BF problems. Additionally, similar to existing works [6][8][13][14], in this study, the number of layers and the number of neurons in each layer are mainly determined by empirical trials, and there is also interest in studying the physical meaning of each layer.
